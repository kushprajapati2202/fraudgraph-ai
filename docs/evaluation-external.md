# FraudGraph AI - Independent test (public dataset)

Rules `rules-v1`, unchanged, run on a dataset the team did not design: [IBM Transactions for Anti Money Laundering, HI-Small](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/ealtman2019/ibm-transactions-for-anti-money-laundering-aml) (CDLA-Sharing-1.0). Generated by `python -I scripts/evaluate_external.py`.

## Why this test exists

[evaluation.md](evaluation.md) measures the rules on scenarios written by the same team that wrote the rules. This test removes that advantage. The data, the laundering patterns and the labels come from IBM's simulator, and no weight, threshold or window was adjusted for it.

## Dataset slice

- Transfers from 2022-09-01 to 2022-09-02: 1,346,480 between 389,873 accounts (the full file has 5,078,345).
- Labeled laundering: 729 transfers, 0.054% of the slice.
- Left out: 522,890 transfers from an account to itself (1 labeled laundering), which the app does not accept.
- Engine time to score the slice in-process: 402 s.

## Transaction-level results

| Threshold | Flagged | TP | FP | FN | Precision | Recall | FPR | Lift over base rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MEDIUM+ (score >= 30) | 90,636 | 88 | 90,548 | 641 | 0.10% | 12.1% | 6.73% | 1.8x |
| HIGH (score >= 60) | 29,943 | 49 | 29,894 | 680 | 0.16% | 6.7% | 2.22% | 3.0x |

Lift is precision divided by the base rate: how much more likely a flagged transfer is to be labeled laundering than one picked at random.

## How to read this

- These numbers are much weaker than the ones in [evaluation.md](evaluation.md). That is the expected, honest result of testing on data the rules were not written for.
- The rules look for money that moves on within 30 to 60 minutes. Many patterns in this dataset are spread over hours or days, so the time-window rules cannot see them.
- The slice is the first days of the file. Accounts therefore have little or no earlier history, which weakens the unusual-amount baseline, and patterns that continue after the slice are cut short.
- Timestamps have minute resolution and there are no device identifiers, so the shared-device rule never fires and transfers forwarded within the same minute are not counted as onward movement.
- Amounts are in 15 currencies and are not converted. The fixed no-baseline threshold of 100,000 was chosen for rupees, so it means something different in each currency here.
- The data is synthetic. It is independent of this project, but it is still not real bank data.

## What this tells us to build next

The gaps point at specific rule changes, not at the AI layer. None of them were made, so this test stays independent.

| Gap seen here | Change to the engine |
|---|---|
| Bipartite, scatter-gather and cycle patterns score 0 to 4% recall | Graph-shape rules over the case graph: cycles back to the origin, many-to-many layers, gather-then-scatter |
| Patterns spread over hours or days | Multi-day linking and onward-movement windows alongside the current 30 to 60 minutes |
| Relationship novelty fires on 36.5% of legitimate transfers | Keep it context-only, and require a longer history before calling a pair new |
| Amounts in 15 currencies | Convert to one currency before the unusual-amount rule |
| Synthetic labels only | Retrain the BigQuery ML model on real labelled outcomes, where its learned view can add recall the rules miss |
