# FraudGraph AI - Evaluation (synthetic data)

Dataset `ds-v2` (seed 2026), rules `rules-v1`, analysis window 60 minutes.
Generated by `python scripts/evaluate.py`. **All figures describe a fictional, self-generated dataset and do not predict real-world performance.**

## Method

- Every transaction carries a ground-truth label (`SUSPICIOUS` or `LEGIT`) from the generator (`data/labels.csv`). Background traffic and lookalike scenarios are `LEGIT`.
- The risk engine never reads labels. Predictions are made at the transaction level.
- *MEDIUM+*: the transaction scored >= 30 and became part of a case. *HIGH*: score >= 60.
- Precision = TP/(TP+FP); recall = TP/(TP+FN); false-positive rate = FP/(FP+TN).

## Dataset

- Transactions: 1539 (22 labeled suspicious)
- Cases created: 6 (HIGH 2, MEDIUM 4); cases containing a labeled-suspicious transaction: 3
- Engine time (score all transactions + build cases, in-process): 22 ms

## Independent test (public dataset)

The same rules, unchanged, were also run on a dataset this team did not design: [IBM Transactions for Anti Money Laundering, HI-Small](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/ealtman2019/ibm-transactions-for-anti-money-laundering-aml) (CDLA-Sharing-1.0). The slice has 1,346,480 transfers, of which 729 (0.054%) are labeled laundering. Full results: [evaluation-external.md](evaluation-external.md).

| Threshold | Flagged | TP | Precision | Recall | FPR | Lift over base rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MEDIUM+ (score >= 30) | 90,636 | 88 | 0.10% | 12.1% | 6.73% | 1.8x |
| HIGH (score >= 60) | 29,943 | 49 | 0.16% | 6.7% | 2.22% | 3.0x |

These figures are far weaker than the ones above. On data they were not written for, a flagged transfer is only about two to three times more likely to be laundering than a random one, mainly because that dataset's patterns unfold over hours or days and the rules watch 30 to 60 minute windows.

## Limitations

- The generator and rules were designed by the same team, so scenarios are easier than real data. The independent test above shows how large that advantage is.
- Lookalike scenarios (legitimate high-value payment, shared family device) are intentionally included and show up as false positives at MEDIUM+; that is expected for a prioritization score.
- Background traffic occasionally produces rapid-onward coincidences; these are reported as false positives.
- Relationship novelty and baselines only use the two-month synthetic window.
- The score is a rule-based prioritization index, not a calibrated probability.
